Cauê Borlina ▶ say it

Gerald H. & Sharon D. Krockover New Frontiers Assistant Professor, Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Sciences, Purdue University.

I study how planets form and evolve, and how those histories shape whether a world can become habitable. My tools are magnetic fields: the faint records locked inside meteorites, ancient crystals, and cave deposits, read with micro‑paleomagnetism.

PhD, Planetary Science, MIT · BSE, Aerospace Engineering & Minor in Physics, University of Michigan

The lab

Purdue Planetary Magnetics Laboratory

We measure magnetic fields frozen into rocks, from grains a few microns across to whole meteorites, to reconstruct the early solar system, planetary dynamos and the conditions for life. We also build VR tools that make geology fieldwork accessible.

Research

From the first million years to the Hadean Earth

Magnetic fields leave records that last billions of years. We use them to test how disks, planets and dynamos worked, and when.

    Outreach & Education

    Bringing the field to everyone

    Purdue · ongoing

    VR in the Geosciences

    A Purdue‑funded initiative bringing virtual reality into geoscience teaching. We're reconstructing a cave environment for the Indiana Department of Natural Resources at the Indiana State Fair, and building classroom experiences from LiDAR scans of Carlsbad Caverns, New Mexico.

    MIT · since 2019

    Minds Across Generations

    Co‑founded an initiative that brings MIT researchers to Lasell Village in Newton, Massachusetts, for 40‑minute talks to older adult learners, a very engaged audience and great practice in science communication.
